FCV Incident Summary
Ten highest-fatality conflict events in Q1 2025 with operational relevance to World Bank programming
⚠ Quarter Highlights

Q1 2025 recorded 1,137 conflict events and 2,760 fatalities across Nigeria — a sharp divergence from Q4 2024: event counts fell 18% but fatalities surged 18%, indicating a shift toward fewer, higher-lethality operations. North West states (Zamfara, Katsina) and Borno State in the North East together accounted for 76% of all fatalities. In Borno, a Nigerian Army operation in Bama LGA on 23 January killed 100 Boko Haram fighters and captured their commander — the single deadliest engagement of the quarter — while ISWAP attacks on civilian farming communities in Kukawa and Damboa killed over 180 people in mid-January. In Zamfara, three major Nigerian Air Force and ground operations between 10–17 March struck militia strongholds in Kaura Namoda LGA, yielding 122 fatalities. North Central bucked the national fatality trend, recording a 49% decline, likely reflecting a seasonal dry-season lull in farmer-herder clashes.

Trend Note

The divergence between falling event counts (-18%) and rising fatalities (+18%) signals a qualitative shift in conflict dynamics: armed groups are conducting fewer but more lethal operations. This pattern is most pronounced in the North East (+59% fatalities despite -24% events) and North West (+41% fatalities, -11% events). The North Central zone is the sole bright spot, with fatalities down 49% — though the March uptick (135 fatalities in March vs 75 in January) suggests the dry-season lull is ending. The South West's 66% fatality increase, while small in absolute terms, warrants close monitoring through Q2.

Section 4 — Analysis
Section 4
Analysis of Key Trends & Emerging Risks
1 North East: Shift to High-Lethality Insurgency

Despite a 24% drop in event count (210 to 160), the North East recorded its highest quarterly fatality total in recent periods: 894 deaths in Q1 2025, up 59% from 564 in Q4 2024. Borno State was the epicentre, accounting for the three largest incidents of the quarter. The pattern of January concentration (587 of 894 NE fatalities occurred in January alone) reflects a surge of coordinated ISWAP and Boko Haram (JAS) operations at the start of the dry season when mobility across Lake Chad marshlands improves.

The 12 January Kukawa cluster (187 fatalities across three simultaneous incidents — two attacks on civilians and one military engagement) represents the most complex multi-target operation recorded this quarter and indicates growing ISWAP operational capacity in the Lake Chad Basin. The 23 January Bama engagement (100 fatalities, BH commander captured) demonstrates continued Nigerian Army offensive capacity but underscores the persistent strength of JAS remnants in southern Borno.

Outlook: Risk remains HIGH through Q2. Dry-season conditions that enabled January operations ease as rains arrive April–May, but displacement of farming communities from Kukawa threatens food security and IDP flows toward Maiduguri.

2 North West: Military Offensives amid Persistent Banditry

The North West was the deadliest zone in Q1 2025, recording 398 events and 1,171 fatalities — 42% of the national total. Zamfara (Kaura Namoda, Shinkafi) and Katsina (Jibia) drove the bulk of violence. The Nigerian Armed Forces conducted multiple high-intensity counter-banditry operations: the 11 January Shinkafi clash (60 fatalities), the 4 January Jibia operation (80 fatalities), and a cluster of three major operations in Kaura Namoda LGA between 10–17 March (122 combined fatalities including an Air Force airstrike on 17 March).

Military operations account for the majority of NW fatalities (Nigerian Armed Forces was the top actor nationally with 1,245 fatalities attributed). While offensives have reduced militia activity in specific corridors, banditry-related kidnapping continues: the 15 March Banga village abduction (56 people, 35 killed) illustrates that civilian communities remain highly exposed despite military pressure.

Outlook: Risk remains HIGH. Monthly fatalities stayed elevated throughout Q1 (Jan: 512, Feb: 292, Mar: 367), with no sign of sustained reduction. Zamfara and Katsina project sites require security corridor assessments before Q2 field missions.

3 North Central: Seasonal Lull — But March Uptick Signals Risk

North Central recorded a 49% decline in fatalities (555 in Q4 2024 to 282 in Q1 2025), the most significant improvement of any zone. This likely reflects the dry-season reduction in farmer-herder competition over water sources and grazing land in Plateau, Benue, Nasarawa and Niger states. Events also fell 27% (252 to 184).

However, March fatalities (135) were nearly double those of January and February (75 and 72 respectively), indicating the lull is ending as agricultural seasons approach. Plateau State historically escalates in April–June as communities compete for farmland. Communal violence in Plateau (Jos, Barkin Ladi, Riyom, Bokkos) and Benue (Guma, Logo) remains the primary driver. Fulani Ethnic Militia was the third-highest fatality actor nationally with 166 deaths attributed in Q1.

Outlook: Risk MEDIUM and rising. April–June represents peak risk window for communal escalation in NC. WB agricultural and livelihood projects in Plateau and Benue require Q2 contingency planning.

4 South East & South South: Persistent Low-Level Violence

The South East (115 events, 172 fatalities) continued to experience IPOB-linked separatist violence, targeted killings of security personnel and government officials, and sit-at-home enforcement in Anambra, Imo, Enugu and Ebonyi. The January spike (118 of 172 SE fatalities) reflects heightened activity at the start of the year; February and March were notably calmer. Fatalities fell 11% quarter-on-quarter, suggesting a modest de-escalation but not a structural change.

The South South (136 events, 126 fatalities) recorded near-identical fatalities to Q4 2024 (-1%), with Rivers, Delta and Edo driving most incidents through cult rivalries, political violence, and sporadic pipeline attacks. A notable February uptick (59 fatalities in Feb vs 23 in Jan) coincided with post-election tensions in Rivers State. The South West saw a disproportionate 66% fatality increase (53 to 88) driven by criminal violence in Lagos and political confrontations in Ogun; event counts rose only 2%, confirming higher lethality per incident.

Outlook: SE and SS risk remains MEDIUM — manageable but requiring vigilance. SW fatality trend warrants close monitoring through Q2.

🔍 Emerging Risk for Q2 2025

Three converging risks are likely to intensify in Q2 (April–June 2025): (1) the approaching rainy season will increase farmer-herder competition in North Central, particularly in Plateau and Benue; (2) the South West's rising fatality trajectory may reflect spill-over of criminal networks from NW banditry into SW logistics corridors — a trend to track through Lagos and Ogun data; and (3) if the Nigerian Army's Kaura Namoda operations displace Zamfara militia networks southward into Kebbi or Niger states, WB projects in those states could face new exposure not seen in prior quarters.

Section 5 — Implications for World Bank Program
Section 5
Implications for World Bank Programming
Identified risks to portfolio, potential mitigation measures, and recommendations for task teams
Portfolio Risk
NE Insurgency: Access Disruption in Borno & Yobe
HIGH RISK

Impact

The January Kukawa cluster (147+ fatalities in a single day) and continued ISWAP/BH operations along the Maiduguri–Baga and Maiduguri–Kukawa corridors directly impede field access for WB-funded projects in agricultural rehabilitation, education and health in Borno and Yobe. Farming communities displaced from Kukawa and Damboa are likely beneficiaries of Lake Chad Basin food security programs; their displacement reduces program reach and distorts targeting data.

Recommended Measures

Activate UNDSS-coordinated security surge protocol for NE field teams. Transition Kukawa and Damboa LGA activities to remote verification and digital payment where feasible. Conduct access corridor reassessment for Q2 supervision missions to Borno no later than April 15, 2025.

NW Banditry: Rural Project Disruption in Zamfara & Katsina
HIGH RISK

Impact

Three major military operations in Kaura Namoda LGA (10, 15, 17 March) and sustained banditry in Zamfara and Katsina create a high-risk environment for WB-funded rural road rehabilitation, irrigation, and agricultural extension activities. Kidnapping-for-ransom operations (e.g., 15 March Banga village: 56 abducted, 35 killed) pose direct threats to project staff and community mobilisers. The 4 January Jibia operation (80 fatalities) signals ongoing conflict in a key cross-border trade corridor.

Recommended Measures

Conduct Conflict Sensitivity Assessment for all NW agricultural and infrastructure projects before Q2 field missions. Establish no-go zone protocols for Kaura Namoda and Shinkafi LGAs pending security stabilisation. Strengthen community liaison protocols with state security agencies (Zamfara KSCWC, Katsina State Security).

NC Seasonal Escalation: Communal Violence in Plateau & Benue
MEDIUM RISK

Impact

Q1 2025 NC fatalities fell 49% in a seasonal dry-season lull, but March's 135 fatalities (vs January's 75) signals the approach of peak communal violence season (April–June). WB rural development, social protection and health projects in Plateau, Benue and Nasarawa states risk disruption during community mobilisation and seasonal agricultural activities when farmer-herder tensions historically peak. The Fulani Ethnic Militia was the third-highest-lethality actor nationally in Q1 (166 fatalities).

Recommended Measures

Build Q2 contingency buffers into NC project implementation plans. Schedule community consultations and beneficiary verification before April rainy season onset. Engage Plateau State Peacebuilding Agency (KSEPA) and relevant LGA Conflict Management Committees for early warning coordination.

SE Sit-at-Home Compliance: Program Delivery Gaps in Anambra, Imo, Enugu
MEDIUM RISK

Impact

Weekly IPOB-enforced sit-at-home orders in Anambra, Imo, Enugu and Ebonyi continue to reduce community mobilisation windows for WB-supported health and education programs. Targeted killings of government officials and security personnel in Q1 (115 events, 172 fatalities) deter local counterpart engagement and reduce state government implementation capacity. January's spike (118 of 172 SE fatalities in January) correlates with heightened enforcement at the start of the year.

Recommended Measures

Reschedule critical program activities outside Monday sit-at-home enforcement windows. Consult with community leaders and local task team coordinators on safe mobilisation periods. Document lost person-days and attendance gaps as security-attributed costs in project reporting and ISR risk ratings.

Portfolio Risk
NE Displacement: IDP Targeting & Service Demand Concentration
MEDIUM RISK

Impact

Continued insurgency-driven displacement in the North East — with an estimated 2.3M+ IDPs nationally concentrated in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe — creates service demand concentration in IDP host communities (particularly Maiduguri and Yola) that outpaces WB social protection, health and education program reach. Fresh displacement from Kukawa and Damboa following Q1 operations will likely not be reflected in current beneficiary targeting lists, leading to exclusion errors in NE project areas.

Recommended Measures

Update beneficiary targeting data to account for Q1 displacement flows from Kukawa and Damboa. Coordinate with IOM DTM and UNHCR on Q2 displacement mapping before next beneficiary registration round. Explore supplemental financing through IDA Crisis Response Window or Emergency Financing windows for IDP host community services.
